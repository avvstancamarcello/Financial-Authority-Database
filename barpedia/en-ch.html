Warning: the fake Swiss lawyer scam

Were you scammed with trading, crypto or a fake bank? Now a "Swiss lawyer" contacts you and says:

"Your capital has been found in a bank in Zurich. It is blocked. To release it you must pay Swiss taxes, transfer costs and bank fees. Pay now or the funds will be confiscated."

This is the second scam. The bait is "Switzerland = safe bank". The aim is to make you pay yet another advance, often in crypto or by foreign transfer.

Before paying, carry out the Effective Verification in 2 minutes.

CH Effective Verification - 3 steps

  1. A - Lawyers: Ask for their full name, firm name, physical address in Switzerland, an email on the firm's own domain, and the canton of registration.
  2. M - Worldwide: Do they say they practise in Zurich? Then they must appear in the Zurich register. Geneva? They must appear in Geneva. In Switzerland the register is cantonal, not national. If they cannot tell you the canton, that is a red flag.
  3. E + V - Effective Verification: Search for the name yourself using the official links below. Do not trust screenshots, PDFs or ID cards they send you. Only the official register counts.

5 red flags of the fake Swiss lawyer

  • Asks for an advance to release funds in a bank: Swiss taxes, transfer costs, FINMA fees, account release. No Swiss bank asks for money like this, through a lawyer on WhatsApp.
  • Uses WhatsApp / Telegram / Gmail: a genuine Swiss firm uses its own domain, a verifiable Swiss landline and a physical address.
  • Cites FINMA as if it were the one releasing the money: FINMA is the financial supervisory authority; it does not release accounts on behalf of private lawyers.
  • Not listed in the cantonal register they gave you: this is the decisive sign. In Switzerland, if you are not in the cantonal register, you cannot practise as a lawyer in that canton.
  • Urgency and threats: "If you do not pay today the bank will confiscate everything." Urgency is the technique used to stop you from checking.

What to do if the lawyer is not listed

  1. Pay nothing. Do not send identity documents, do not sign any engagement letter, do not give an IBAN / wallet.
  2. Keep everything: emails, messages, phone number, and the IBAN / wallet they gave you to pay.
  3. Report it: in Switzerland to Fedpol - Fraud reporting → and, in Italy, to the Polizia Postale. Bring this page with you as a record of the check you made.
  4. Check the original intermediary: go back to the AMEV Financial Authority Database and check whether the platform that first scammed you was authorised in Switzerland by FINMA. Also read the AMEV Equivalence page to understand the method.
