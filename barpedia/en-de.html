Warning: the fake German lawyer scam

Were you scammed with trading, crypto or a fake bank? Now a "lawyer from Berlin" or "from Munich" contacts you and says:

"We have found your capital in Germany. To start the recovery you must pay legal fees, German taxes and a deposit for the court. Pay now or the proceedings will be closed."

This is the second scam. The bait is the recovery of your stolen capital. The aim is to make you pay yet another advance. The word "Germany" is used to give an impression of seriousness and precision.

Before paying, carry out the Effective Verification in 2 minutes.

DE Effective Verification – 3 steps

  1. A – Lawyers: Ask for their full first and last name, Kanzlei name, physical address in Germany, an email on the Kanzlei's own domain, and the Kammer they belong to.
  2. M – Worldwide: Do they claim to practise in Berlin? Then they must be registered with the Rechtsanwaltskammer Berlin. Munich? They must be listed with the Rechtsanwaltskammer München. In Germany, registration is with the regional Kammer.
  3. E + V – Effective Verification: Search for the first and last name yourself in the federal BRAK register below. Do not trust screenshots, PDFs or ID cards they send you. Only the official register counts. Consultation is free for everyone.

Official verification – direct links to German sources

Click and search for the name. If they are not listed as zugelassen, do not pay.

1. Bundesweites Amtliches Anwaltsverzeichnis – BRAK – Federal source

The official federal register. The Bundesrechtsanwaltskammer maintains the Gesamtverzeichnis with all lawyers admitted in Germany, European lawyers admitted in Germany and Rechtsbeistände. The regional Kammern enter the data. It serves to tell whether a person is zugelassen, where they have their Kanzlei and which Kammer they belong to. It is not meant to recommend a lawyer; it is meant to verify whether they exist.

Check here:
BRAK – Bundesweites Amtliches Anwaltsverzeichnis – Search for a Rechtsanwalt →

What to check: exact last and first name, Kanzlei name, address, email, Kammer, date of Zulassung, any Fachanwalt titles. If the name is not there, or the email is Gmail / Proton / Outlook, stop.

2. BRAK – Bundesrechtsanwaltskammer – Institutional website

To understand the system and find the list of regional Kammern.

Check here:

3. Berlin – Rechtsanwaltskammer Berlin

In Berlin the Kammer offers a lawyer search by area of law, district and foreign languages. The information is declared by the lawyers themselves, so always cross-check it with the federal BRAK register.

Check here:

4. Munich – Rechtsanwaltskammer München

The largest Kammer in Germany, with over 22,000 members. Responsible for lawyers whose Kanzlei is in the OLG München district. Bavaria has three Kammern: Bamberg, München and Nürnberg.

5 red flags of the fake German lawyer

  • Asks for an advance to start the recovery: security deposit, German taxes, court costs, BaFin commissions to be paid in crypto, by foreign transfer or by card.
  • Uses WhatsApp / Telegram / Gmail: a genuine German Kanzlei uses its own domain, a verifiable German landline and a physical address with the Kanzlei.
  • Cites BaFin as if they were the one releasing the money: BaFin is the financial supervisory authority; it does not release accounts on behalf of private lawyers.
  • Not listed in the Bundesweites Amtliches Anwaltsverzeichnis: this is the decisive sign. If they are not listed as zugelassen, they are not a lawyer authorised to practise in Germany.
  • Urgency and threats: "If you do not pay today the court will close the case." Urgency is the technique used to stop you from checking.

What to do if the lawyer is not listed

  1. Pay nothing. Do not send identity documents, do not sign any engagement letter, do not give out an IBAN / wallet.
  2. Keep everything: emails, messages, phone number, and the IBAN / wallet they gave you to pay.
  3. Report it: in Germany to the local police and at BKA – Bundeskriminalamt →, and in Italy to the Polizia Postale. Bring this page with you as a record of the check you made. You can also report to the Kammer the fake lawyer claims to belong to.
  4. Check the original intermediary: go back to the AMEV Financial Authority Database and check whether the platform that first scammed you was authorised in Germany by BaFin. Also read the AMEV Equivalence page to understand the method.
